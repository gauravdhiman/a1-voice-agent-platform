## The short answer

Most small businesses pay somewhere between $49 and $349 a month for an AI receptionist. That is the range where the serious products for small service businesses live. Below $49, you are usually looking at a voicemail upgrade or a very basic call menu, not a real receptionist. Above $350 or so, you are into enterprise plans built for call centers and multi-location chains.

But the monthly number is only half the story. Two plans can both say "$99 a month" and cost you wildly different amounts by the end of the year, because what is included and what counts as billable changes from vendor to vendor. That is what the rest of this post is about.

Flat monthly is the most common for small businesses, and for good reason: it is predictable. You know what you are paying in January, and you know what you are paying in December. Per-minute pricing can look cheaper at first glance, but it punishes you for success. The month your marketing works and call volume spikes is exactly the month your phone bill does too.

## What you actually get at each price point

So what does the money buy? Here is roughly how the market breaks down, from someone who has read a lot of these spec sheets.

**Under $49 a month.** This is the budget tier, and honestly, most of it is not a real AI receptionist. You get smart voicemail, call routing menus, maybe a chatbot that can take a message. It does not hold a natural conversation, it does not book into your calendar, and it does not know your pricing. If all you need is "someone picks up and takes a message," this works. If you want calls handled, it will disappoint you.

**$49 to $149 a month.** This is the sweet spot for most small businesses, and it is where our own plans live. You get a real conversational AI that answers in your business name, answers common questions from your actual service and pricing info, books appointments into your calendar, takes messages and orders, and texts you a summary after each call. For a [dental office](/dental) that needs appointment booking or an [HVAC company](/hvac) that needs after-hours emergency triage, this tier covers it.

**$150 to $349 a month.** You get everything in the tier above plus deeper integrations: CRM logging, dispatch workflows, payment collection, multi-location support, and higher included call volumes. This is built for businesses doing real call volume, like plumbing companies in storm season or restaurants taking high volumes of reservations and takeout orders.

**$350 and up.** Enterprise territory. Custom voice models, dedicated onboarding teams, SLA guarantees, compliance features for healthcare and finance, integrations with specialized software. If you are a single-location shop, you almost certainly do not need this. If someone tries to sell it to you anyway, get a second quote.

## The hidden costs nobody puts in the headline

The monthly price is the number vendors advertise. These are the numbers that show up later.

**Setup and onboarding fees.** Some vendors charge $200 to $1,000 for what they call "concierge setup" or "voice design." That might be fair if they are genuinely doing the work, but ask what it covers. Our setup is included in the plan and takes about 48 hours, because I think charging you extra to start using the product is backwards. Not every vendor agrees.

**Overage charges.** On flat plans with included minutes, the overage rate is the number that matters most. I have seen vendors advertise a cheap $49 plan with only 100 included minutes and then charge $0.40 a minute after that. At 300 minutes a month, your "$49 plan" costs $129. Always ask: how many minutes are included, and what does minute 101 cost?

**Add-on modules.** Appointment booking, CRM integration, call recording, extra phone lines, additional locations. Some vendors sell these separately, and each one adds $20 to $60 a month. By the time you add the features you actually wanted, the plan costs double the headline price. Ask for the all-in number for your use case, not the base number.

**Number and line costs.** If you need a new local number, most vendors include one. If you want to keep your existing number and forward it, that is usually free too, but some charge for porting. Minor, but worth asking about so there are no surprises.

**Contracts and cancellation fees.** The cheapest-looking annual plans often lock you in for 12 months with a cancellation fee. Month-to-month costs a little more but keeps the vendor honest. I always recommend starting month-to-month. If the product works, the annual discount is still there later.

The answering service column deserves a closer look, because it is the most common comparison. Answering services love to advertise a low base rate, like $59 a month. Then the fine print kicks in: that covers 50 calls, every call after that is a dollar or more, and their definition of a "call" includes the 90 seconds their agent spent figuring out which business the caller wanted. ## What moves your bill up or down

Not every business pays the same amount for the same product. The five things that move your price:

**Call volume.** This is the big one. A salon getting 60 calls a month needs a different plan than a plumbing company getting 600. Be honest about your volume when you sign up. Most vendors can look at your first month and tell you whether you are on the right plan.

**Average call length.** On per-minute plans, a chatty customer base costs you more. Booking-heavy businesses have longer calls than simple question-and-answer businesses. This is another reason I prefer flat pricing: a thorough call that books a $500 job should not cost you extra.

**Features you turn on.** Every integration you add, every location you add, every workflow you build can nudge the price up. Start with the core features you need, then add. You can always turn more on later.

**Business hours vs 24/7.** Some vendors charge more for round-the-clock coverage. After-hours calls are where AI receptionists earn their keep though. Most missed revenue happens outside business hours, so skimping on coverage to save $30 a month is usually false economy.

**Number of lines and locations.** One location with one number is the base case. Each additional location typically adds to the bill. If you are multi-location, ask about bundle pricing before you assume it is per-location list price times three.

## Questions to ask before you pay anything

Whatever vendor you are looking at, us included, ask these before you sign anything.

1. What exactly is included in the monthly price, and what costs extra?
2. How many minutes or calls are included, and what does the overage cost?
3. Is there a setup fee, and what does the setup actually involve?
4. Can I update the AI's knowledge myself, or do I have to file a ticket every time my hours change?
5. What happens when the AI cannot handle a call? How fast does the handoff reach me?
6. Is there a trial or pilot so I can hear it handle real calls before I commit?
7. What is the contract term? Can I cancel month to month?
8. How long does setup actually take, from signup to answering live calls?

The vendors who answer these clearly are the ones worth your money. The ones who dodge them are telling you something too.
