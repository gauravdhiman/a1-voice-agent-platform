## The short answer: about an hour, live within 48 hours

Let me put the number in context, because an hour sounds either suspiciously fast or annoyingly slow depending on what you are comparing it to. Hiring a receptionist takes weeks: write the job post, read resumes, interview, make an offer, wait for a start date, then train them for a few more weeks before they know your services and your regulars. A traditional phone system takes days of quotes, a technician visit, and a manual nobody reads. An AI receptionist is closer to setting up a new email account than to hiring a person. You answer questions about your business, you test it with a few calls, and it starts answering.

The 48 hours part is the calendar, not the work. Once you finish the setup, the number needs to be pointed at the system, and that can mean a call forward, a new number, or a port of your existing number. Porting an existing number is the slowest piece, and it is controlled by your current carrier, not by us. So the realistic promise is this: about an hour of your time, and your first real caller gets answered within two days. If you use a new number or simple call forwarding instead of porting, you can be live the same day.

That is the setup for a typical small business: one location, one phone number, one calendar, a handful of services. If that sounds like your business, stop worrying about the timeline. The setup is not the hard part. The hard part is the decision to try it, and you have already made that decision by reading this far.

## The setup, step by step

Here is what the hour actually looks like. I am going to walk through it the way we do it with a new customer, because the steps are the same whether you set it up yourself or we do it with you on a call.

Step one takes the longest, and it is the only step that really needs you. The AI needs to know what you sell, what it costs, when you are open, and where you are. A [dental office](/dental) needs to list cleanings, fillings, and emergency visits, plus whether you take new patients. An [HVAC company](/hvac) needs to list installs, repairs, and maintenance visits, plus the after-hours emergency rule. A [plumbing company](/plumbing) needs to list the services and the rough price ranges callers always ask about. You know all of this already. Saying it out loud takes 20 minutes. Writing it down first takes longer, which is why I tell owners not to prepare. Just show up and talk.

Step two is the rules, and this is where owners overthink. You do not need a perfect rulebook. You need four answers: what should the AI book on its own, what should it take a message for, when should it transfer the caller to you, and what counts as an emergency. Everything else can wait until you have heard a week of real calls. The rules you set on day one are a starting point, not a contract.

Step three is the calendar, and it is five minutes if your calendar is where it should be. Connect Google Calendar or Outlook, pick which calendar takes bookings, and set your working hours. If your bookings live on a paper diary or in someone's head, this step takes longer, and it is worth fixing anyway, because the AI can only book into a calendar it can see.

Step four is the phone number. This is the step with the most options, and I will go through them below, because the choice you make here is what decides whether you are live today or in two days.

Step five is the fun part. You call your own number and pretend to be a customer. Book an appointment. Ask a question you know the answer to. Try to confuse it. Then listen to the recordings and fix the two or three things that sound off. Most owners do three or four test calls and they are done. The greeting gets tuned, the transfer rule gets adjusted, and the system is ready for real callers.

## Your phone number: the one choice that changes the timeline

Everything else in the setup is under your control. The phone number is the one piece that involves another company, and it is where the timeline stretches. You have three options, and each one has a different speed.

Call forwarding is the fastest. Your existing number stays exactly where it is, with your current carrier, and you set it to forward to the AI number when you do not pick up, or all the time. This takes minutes to switch on, your customers keep calling the same number they have always called, and you can be live today. The trade-off is that you are still paying your old carrier for the number, and the forwarding setup is one more thing to maintain. For most businesses trying the system for the first time, this is the right start, because it is reversible in minutes.

Porting is the cleanest, and the slowest. You move your existing number over to the AI platform, so the number itself lives there. Callers notice nothing. The catch is that porting is a regulated process, your current carrier has to release the number, and that takes one to two business days in most cases, sometimes longer if the account details do not match. If you want to port, start the setup now and let the port run in the background while you do the rest. Do not wait for the port before doing steps one through three.

A new number is the simplest of all. The platform gives you a fresh local number, you put it on your website and your Google Business Profile, and you are live in minutes. The trade-off is obvious: your existing customers do not know the new number yet. This works well for a new business, or for a business that mostly gets calls from Google and its website rather than from repeat customers dialing a number they memorized years ago.

My advice is simple. If you want to start today, use call forwarding. If you want the cleanest long-term setup and you can wait two days, port the number. Either way, do not let the number decision delay the rest of the setup. The business details, the rules, and the calendar can all be done while the number is sorting itself out.

## What actually takes the time

If setup is only an hour, why do some businesses take a week? It is never the technology. It is one of five things, and all five are fixable before you start.

The first is the service list. Some businesses know their services cold and can rattle them off in five minutes. Others have a website that lists twelve services, a price sheet that lists eight, and a receptionist who books three more that appear nowhere. Reconciling that takes a conversation, and it is worth having, because the AI can only answer questions about services it knows. If your own team cannot agree on what you sell, your callers are already confused too.

The second is calendar access. The AI needs to see your real availability, and that means connecting the calendar that actually holds your bookings. In a lot of small businesses, that calendar lives on the owner's personal Google account, or on a front-desk computer nobody else can log into, or in two places at once. Sorting out which calendar is the real one takes a few emails and a password reset. Do it before setup day and the step takes five minutes.

The third is the approval chain. In a one-owner business, setup is fast, because the person answering the questions is the person who decides. In a business with two partners, or a manager who needs the owner's sign-off on the greeting, every small decision becomes a round trip. If more than one person has a say, get them in the same room for the setup hour. It saves a week of back and forth.

The fourth is over-customizing the voice. Some owners want to hear ten versions of the greeting before they let a real caller hear one. The greeting matters, but it is not permanent. You can change it in a minute, and you will change it after you hear real calls anyway. Pick a greeting that sounds like you, make three test calls, and ship it. The callers will tell you what to fix.

The fifth is the integration wish list. Owners sometimes treat setup day as the day to connect everything: the CRM, the payment system, the review tool, the dispatch software. Those integrations are real and useful, but they are not setup. They are week-two work. Get the phone answering first, then add one integration at a time, starting with the one that saves you the most time.

## Your first week: what to expect after setup

Setup day is the beginning, not the end. The first week is when the system learns your business for real, and it is worth knowing what that week looks like so you do not panic on day two.

Day one is test calls and the first real callers. Listen to the first five calls, or read the transcripts. You will find two or three things to fix immediately: a service name the AI pronounces wrong, a question it answers with the old price, a transfer rule that is too eager or too slow. Fix those on day one. They take minutes each.

Days two and three are the tuning window. This is when you hear the calls you did not expect: the caller who asks about a service you forgot to list, the one who wants to reschedule instead of book, the one who asks for you by name. Add the missing service, add the reschedule rule, add the name. Each fix is small, and each one makes the next hundred calls better.

By the end of week one, the system has handled a full week of your real call patterns, and the fixes slow down. You check the call log once a day instead of after every call. You stop listening to every recording and start skimming the summaries. That is the system settling in. From here, the maintenance is a few minutes a week: a new service, a price change, a holiday schedule. The setup hour is the big investment. After that, it is upkeep.

## How to make setup fast

If you want the fastest possible setup, here is what I would do, in order. None of this is hard, and all of it can be done before the setup call.

First, write down your services and prices on one page. Not a brochure, just a list: the service, the price or price range, and how long it takes. If you have that list, step one takes ten minutes instead of twenty.

Second, decide the four call rules before you start. What should the AI book on its own? What should it take a message for? When should it transfer to you? What counts as an emergency? Four answers, written on the same page as the services.

Third, make sure you can log into the calendar that holds your real bookings. If it is on someone else's account, get access sorted now. If you have two calendars, pick the one that wins.

Fourth, decide the number approach. Forwarding for today, porting for the clean long-term setup. If you choose porting, have your current carrier account number and the name on the account ready, because the port request needs them to match.

Fifth, book an hour with no interruptions. Not an hour between jobs, not an hour while you are also answering the phone. One hour, phone on silent, door closed. The setup is fast because it is focused. The moment it competes with the rest of your day, it stretches into a week.

Do those five things and you will be live faster than almost anyone else. Skip them and you will still be live, just slower, because the work still has to happen somewhere. The setup is not hard. It is just a list of small decisions about a business you already run.
