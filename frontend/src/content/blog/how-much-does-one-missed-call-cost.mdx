Here is a question I ask every small business owner I meet: what happens when a customer calls and nobody picks up?

Most of them shrug. Voicemail, they say. They will call back.

They usually do not. Study after study lands in the same depressing neighborhood: most callers who hit voicemail never call back. They call your competitor instead. Think about your own behavior. When was the last time you left a voicemail for a business and waited patiently? Exactly.

## Let us do the math

Say you are a [plumber](/plumbing). An average job is worth, conservatively, 350 dollars. Say you miss three calls a week because you are under a sink or on a ladder. That is 156 missed calls a year. If even one in five of those callers would have become a paying job, that is 31 jobs. At 350 dollars each, that is nearly 11,000 dollars a year walking out the door. And those are conservative numbers. For an [HVAC company](/hvac) where a system replacement runs into the thousands, one missed call can be a five-figure loss.

[Dental offices](/dental), same story, different numbers. A new patient is worth anywhere from 500 to 1,500 dollars in first-year revenue. Miss two new-patient calls a week and you are potentially leaving 50,000 to 150,000 dollars a year on the table. I am not saying every missed call is a new patient. But some of them are, and you never find out which ones, because they booked with the practice across town.

[Restaurants](/restaurants) feel it on Friday nights. The phone rings during the dinner rush, nobody can step away, and that is a 60 dollar order that went to the place with the online ordering button. Multiply by every Friday. It adds up fast.

## The hidden cost: the ad spend you already paid

Here is the part nobody talks about. The cost is not just the lost job. It is the ad spend you already paid to make that phone ring. If you spend 1,000 dollars a month on Google Ads and 20 percent of those calls go unanswered, you just lit 200 dollars on fire every month. You paid for the ring and fumbled the catch.

## Three ways to fix it

You have three options, and I will be straight about all of them.

**Hire someone.** A part-time receptionist costs 15 to 20 dollars an hour, and they still cannot cover nights and weekends unless you pay a lot more. Fine if you have the call volume, expensive if you do not.

**Use an answering service.** A human picks up and takes a message. Better than voicemail, but they cannot book into your calendar or answer questions about your prices. And the per-call fees add up.

**Get an AI receptionist.** It answers in seconds, 24 hours a day, books straight into your calendar, answers common questions, and texts you a summary. Plans start around 49 dollars a month, which is less than one missed plumbing job. I am biased here, obviously, since this is what we build. But run your own numbers and see.

The real point is not which option you pick. It is that missed calls have a price tag, and most businesses have never calculated it. Do the math for your business this week. Count your missed calls for seven days, multiply by your average job value, multiply by a conservative close rate. If the number does not bother you, congratulations, you do not have this problem. If it does, you know what to fix first.
