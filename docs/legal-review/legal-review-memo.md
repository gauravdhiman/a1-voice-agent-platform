## Terms — what was missing and what I did

### Critical (would have left you unprotected)

1. **No governing law, venue, liability cap, or indemnity — all blank.** A Terms without a liability cap means a single unhappy customer can claim unlimited consequential damages (lost business because a call was missed). **Fixed:** Arizona law, Maricopa County courts, 30-day informal resolution first, liability capped at 12 months of fees paid ($100 floor for free plan), consequential damages excluded, and a customer indemnity covering exactly your biggest risk — claims arising from *their* agent instructions, *their* communications, and *their* failure to get consent. I deliberately did **not** add arbitration or a jury waiver: for a solo founder selling to SMBs, arbitration is usually more expensive and slower than your home courts, and you'd lose the home-field advantage.
2. **Billing section was an empty promise** ("must be stated at checkout") plus a reviewer TODO. Auto-renewal without clear disclosure is the single most-litigated clause in SaaS (FTC negative-option rule and state auto-renewal laws). **Fixed with your actual model:** plan ladder (Free / $49 / $149 / $299), annual = 10-for-12, credits (2,500 / 10,000 / 21,000, 10 credits per minute rounded up), no rollover, top-up packs, auto-renewal with 30 days' notice of price increases, cancellation effective at end of period, a defined refund list (law-required, termination without cause, uncured material breach, pilot terms), and a 7-day grace period before suspension for failed payment. Also added the honest rule: no charges beyond plan + top-ups/upgrades/overage the customer agreed to, and calls may pause when credits run out.
3. **No IP-infringement protection for you or the customer.** Added a two-way setup: customer indemnifies you (above); you indemnify the customer if the Service itself infringes someone's IP, with the standard modify/replace/terminate remedy.

### Material (specific to an AI voice product)

4. **Recording and consent duties were one vague sentence.** Two-party-consent states (CA, FL, etc.), the FCC rule that AI voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, and state AI-disclosure laws are where voice-agent companies actually get sued — usually the *business customer*, who then comes after the platform. **Fixed:** Section 3 now names TCPA/FCC, recording consent, opt-out honoring, and AI disclosure (never claim to be human when asked), and puts all of it squarely on the customer, who controls the configuration. It also separates inbound (your core, low risk) from outbound (reminders/notifications — consent required, no blast robocalling without written approval) and covers caller ID and SMS/A2P 10DLC duties.
5. **The PHI ban conflicted with your own dental vertical — flagged, not hidden.** The draft banned PHI outright, but dental is a launch vertical and appointment details for a dental practice can be PHI. **Fixed as honestly as possible today:** Terms now say no BAA is currently offered, agents must not collect clinical/health information, and appointment logistics are limited to name/contact/scheduling with the customer accepting the remaining risk in writing in their order. **Your decision needed (see below):** either stay out of clinical data and keep dental to front-desk logistics only (current position), or do HIPAA properly later (paid, BAA-capable tiers from Twilio/Google/etc. + a signed BAA). Do not market "HIPAA compliant" anywhere until the second path is done.
6. **Missing operational clauses, now added:** no-SLA statement; 30-day data export window after termination, then deletion; survival clause; free/pilot plan withdrawal; DMCA notice process; assignment (including your right to assign on a sale of the business); entire-agreement and signed-DPA/order precedence; feedback license; security duties for accounts (accurate info, MFA, removing ex-users).

## Privacy Policy — what was missing and what I did

### Critical

1. **No named subprocessors.** The draft listed only categories, while its own internal notes named the real stack. For a company whose callers' voices go to third parties, an unnamed list is both a trust problem and a problem under Google's API policy and state privacy laws. **Fixed:** Section 4 now names them, framed accurately as "depending on configuration": Google Cloud, Supabase, Oracle Cloud, Cloudflare/R2; Twilio; Gemini, OpenAI, Deepgram, Cartesia, Fish Audio, Cerebras, OpenRouter; Stripe; Resend; Telegram (only where a customer configures it); PostHog (optional analytics); and customer-directed tools (Google Calendar/Gmail/Sheets, HubSpot, etc.).
2. **THE GEMINI PROBLEM — the most important finding in this review.** The draft's own notes flag that on Google's **unpaid** Gemini API tier, Google may use submitted data to improve its products and humans may review it. If any production call traffic runs on an unpaid Gemini tier, the policy's "we don't train on your data" promise is false through your subprocessor, and callers' voices could be human-reviewed by Google. **Action before publishing (checklist #1): confirm in the Google Cloud console that the production Gemini project is on a paid tier with no-training terms. If it isn't, switch it before a single paying customer's calls flow through it.** The final Policy is written to commit you to no-training configurations only.
3. **No Google Limited Use disclosure.** Your product connects Gmail/Calendar/Sheets. Google's API Services User Data Policy *requires* a specific disclosure in your privacy policy (use only for user-facing features, no advertising use, no human reading except narrow exceptions). It was absent; without it you risk OAuth verification failure or revocation. **Fixed:** new Section 5 with that disclosure.
4. **No retention numbers.** "As long as reasonably needed" alone reads evasive and fails the specificity state regulators expect. **Fixed with your actual defaults:** recordings **90 days** (from the production audit in the draft notes), then auto-deleted; account/call records deleted or de-identified within 90 days of closure; billing records 7 years; security logs 12 months; support 3 years.

### Material

5. **No cookies/analytics section at all**, despite optional PostHog in production and no consent banner. **Fixed:** Section 8 — necessary cookies, optional analytics, no ad/tracking cookies, browser controls, and honoring Global Privacy Control. Because you're US-focused, no banner is legally required today; the Policy says optional analytics gets disabled on request for regions requiring consent.
6. **No biometrics statement.** Voice products sit one feature away from Illinois BIPA liability. **Fixed:** explicit "we do not create voiceprints or identify speakers by voice."
7. **Rights section was generic.** **Fixed:** named rights (know/access, correct, delete, portability, opt-out of sale/share — restating you do neither — limit sensitive use, non-discrimination, authorized agents), a 45-day response clock, an appeal path, and the correct routing rule (callers go to the business that controls their data; you support the business).
8. **Also added:** breach-notification commitment (notify customers promptly so *they* can meet duties to their callers); no-automated-legal-decisions statement; security specifics limited to claims that are actually true (encryption in transit, encryption at rest via providers, access controls); under-13 language tightened; demo conversations on your own website brought inside the Policy's scope (the draft arguably didn't cover a visitor talking to a website demo).

## Decisions I made for you (change any with one word)

- **No mandatory arbitration, no class-action waiver, no jury waiver.** Home-court (Maricopa County) B2B litigation + informal resolution first. Simpler and cheaper for a solo founder.
- **Liability cap = 12 months of fees.** The industry standard; anything lower looks unserious to bigger customers, anything higher is needless exposure.
- **Home address not published.** Your ACC registered address is public record, but there's no reason to put your street address on a marketing website. Email-first notice + ACC reference is legally sufficient for these documents.
- **Dental = logistics only, no clinical data, no BAA offered yet.** (Section 3(f) of the Terms.)
